THE AMERICAN POSITION: December 1st, 1935

Mr. President, you command a continental superpower at a pivotal moment.

Your Strengths:

Vast industrial capacity and natural resources
Isolated from European turmoil by two oceans
Growing Pacific presence (General MacArthur in the Philippines)
Strong economy recovering from depression

The World Stage: Europe teeters on instability—Germany rearms aggressively, Italy eyes Ethiopia, Japan consolidates Asia. Britain and France remain dominant but stretched thin.

My Immediate Counsel:

Fortify the Pacific — Japan's expansion poses the gravest long-term threat. Strengthen Philippine defenses and deepen ties with Britain and China.

Monitor Europe — Avoid entanglement, but maintain diplomatic channels. Economic leverage beats military commitment.

Build Naval Capacity — Your greatest asset. Expand the fleet discreetly; naval supremacy will determine your future influence.

Cultivate Latin America — Secure your hemisphere as a stable base while Old World powers clash.

What would you prefer to prioritize first—strengthening Pacific defenses, diplomatic positioning in Europe, or economic expansion at home?

SITUATION REPORT: December 8th, 1935

Mr. President, developments have accelerated dramatically since our last consultation.

Critical Updates:

✦ You've already acted decisively — Military expansion in California, Texas, and Florida is complete. Weapons flow to China underway. Naval construction authorized. Bold moves.

✦ Your covert North Africa operations are working — French colonial infrastructure crumbles. But keep them deniable; exposure invites British backlash.

✦ The Pacific remains your vulnerability — Japan consolidates Manchuria. Germany rearms. Time is compressing.

My Immediate Recommendations:

1. Reinforce the Philippines NOW — MacArthur needs more than one battalion. This is your forward position against Japan's ambitions.

2. Accelerate naval construction — Your new carrier and battleship cannot arrive fast enough. Push production.

3. Maintain deniability in North Africa — Continue sabotage, but do NOT claim credit. Let France blame insurgents.

4. Signal to Britain cautiously — You're subtly repositioning globally. Britain notices. Use this leverage diplomatically.

What is your priority: reinforcing MacArthur's garrison, or deepening North African operations further?

SITUATION REPORT: December 15th, 1935

Mr. President, the world has transformed in one week. Your North African invasion has shocked the global order.

What You've Accomplished: ✦ Casablanca, Oran, Algiers—all American. ✦ French military collapsed. Paris reeling politically. ✦ Global powers reassessing American intentions. ✦ Your military demonstrated overwhelming superiority.

The Price: ✦ Britain concerned (but cautious—needs you against Germany). ✦ France hostile. Italy alarmed. Germany protesting. ✦ Isolationist Americans questioning your aggression. ✦ China sensing opportunity—demanding more weapons.

Your Critical Decision Now:

You control North Africa, but you've revealed your hand. Three paths forward:

1. CONSOLIDATE & STABILIZE — Secure these territories, establish governance, signal peaceful intentions. Prevents broader conflict.

2. EXPAND FURTHER — Strike at French West Africa or push deeper Mediterranean. Seize momentum while enemies regroup.

3. PIVOT PACIFIC — Reinforce Philippines heavily, ignore Europe, prepare for inevitable Japan confrontation.

My Counsel: Consolidate North Africa first. You've shocked the world—overreach invites coalition against you. Strengthen MacArthur in the Philippines with 2-3 new battalions. Signal to Britain you're a stabilizing force, not a rogue power.

What's your move—hold position, expand in Africa, or shift focus to the Pacific?
