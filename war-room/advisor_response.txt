Mr. President, The Tide Turns—But The Storm Approaches

As of December 21st, 1935, you control a fractured empire.

Your Military Reality

Kyushu (Japan): Your 1st Amphibious Assault Division bleeds. 50% casualties. Japanese counteroffensives intensify daily. Supply lines under constant naval attack. This invasion is sustainable but NOT winnable without dramatic change.

Indochina (French): 2nd Marine Expeditionary Force holds coastal zones but stalls in the highlands. French resistance proves stubborn and tactically competent. Nationalist movements prove unreliable.

Syria (French): 3rd Marine Expeditionary Force controls Damascus and urban centers, but French mountain strongholds remain defiant.

The Catastrophic Problem

BRITAIN HAS TURNED AGAINST YOU. Royal Navy warships now protect French interests in the Mediterranean. Your logistics are strangled. American naval commanders report "significant concern" about British intervention.

Germany proposes a Tripartite Axis alliance. Japan and Italy are listening.

My Stark Assessment

You cannot win three simultaneous wars. Casualty reports have shocked Washington. Congress questions your strategy. Industrial production, while surging, cannot sustain this pace indefinitely.

IMMEDIATE ORDERS

CONSOLIDATE, DO NOT EXPAND:

Hold Kyushu beachheads; cease offensive operations
Fortify Syria and Indochina positions; prepare defensive lines
Withdraw 3rd Marine Expeditionary Force from Syria toward Atlantic—prepare for potential British naval confrontation

DIPLOMATIC OFFENSIVE:

Open secret negotiations with Britain immediately. Propose division of spoils: you keep Syria/Indochina, Britain keeps French Africa
Isolate France diplomatically. Offer harsh but survivable peace terms

INDUSTRIAL REALITY:

You have 3-4 months before current production cannot sustain three theaters

What is your priority, Mr. President?
